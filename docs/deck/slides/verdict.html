THE VERDICT

Classical wins today. Use the exact solver for TVA’s placement.

What we found

At 200 samples on 23-qubit pieces, QAOA’s piece gap was 0.4 versus 2.4 for random. Give random and DE the same total budget (30,200 samples) and the gap closes.

What we did not find

No speedup or quality win over the exact ILP, which solves IEEE 300 in about 17 ms.

Where it could matter

Hypotheses, not results: larger N-1, weighted and budgeted variants, and better hardware.

For TVA: the real cost is installation ($40k–$180k per PMU), communications and timing, not choosing the locations.

Close on honesty. We found no end-to-end quantum advantage and we say so; the track README warns against crediting quantum for what the ILP already does. The defensible claim: with a small sample budget QAOA is a much better sampler than random search on a 23-qubit space, and once tuning cost is counted fairly classical sampling catches up. Piece-gap numbers are from results/hybrid_big.json and hybrid_fair.json. Next steps if we continue: QAOA versions of N-1 and budget, shot-based tuning on the 6-bus piece, and a priced QPU run.

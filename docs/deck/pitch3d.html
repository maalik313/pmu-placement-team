Where should a power company put its grid sensors?

And can a quantum computer help decide?

Team Tiger Qubits · November 13–15, 2026

1 · The problem

A power company has to see its whole grid, live.

  • WHATSensors called PMUs read the grid many times a second. Each costs $40k–$180k to install.
  • GOALUse as few as possible and still watch every substation. On our 14-substation test grid that is 4.
  • CATCHFour is fragile. Lose one sensor and 4 substations go dark. Watching each one twice takes 9 sensors.
4sensors, cheapest9sensors, safest

3 · What we built

A quantum search, double-checked by classical math.

  • METHODEach substation is a yes or no: sensor or not. A quantum algorithm (QAOA) tries many combinations at once and favors the good ones.
  • BUILDShrink the grid, split it into pieces, search each piece, repair, then a checker proves every substation is covered.
  • STATUSWorks on simulators and two real quantum chips (Rigetti, IonQ). Unproven: any win over the classical exact solver.

IonQ Forte hardware run: completed · 13.5% optimal

5 · What we learned

Classical wins today. Quantum has a signal.

Proved

The exact solver finds the true best answer for grids up to 300 substations in milliseconds.

Surprised us

No quantum speedup. Count the tuning cost fairly and random search catches up.

Next

Harder versions, like seeing every substation twice, on bigger grids and better chips.

Utilities should use the exact solver today. Here is where quantum could matter next.
